Artificial intelligence · 4 min read

AI is moving from chatting to doing

For a few years, AI tools mostly answered questions. Now the focus is on agents: AI systems that take a goal, break it into steps and use software on your behalf, like booking a trip, fixing code or sorting a spreadsheet.

Why it matters

An assistant that only talks saves you reading time. An agent that works can save you hours. Companies are rushing to connect AI to calendars, email, browsers and developer tools.

The catch

Agents make mistakes, and a mistake that takes action costs more than a wrong answer. Good products keep a human in the loop for important steps and show exactly what the AI did.

Hardware · 3 min read

Your next laptop and phone will think on their own

Modern devices now include an NPU, a chip built for AI work. It runs AI features right on your device instead of sending your data to a distant server.

Faster and more private

Local AI means live captions, photo editing and writing help can work with no internet, with lower delay, and your files stay on your device.

What to look for

When buying a device, check how much memory it has and whether it has an NPU. AI features run best on devices with enough of both.

Emerging tech · 4 min read

Quantum computing: still early, but getting real

Normal computers use bits that are 0 or 1. Quantum computers use qubits, which can hold a mix of both states. For certain problems, this lets them explore many possibilities at once.

Where it could help

Researchers hope for progress in drug discovery, new materials and complex optimisation, like planning delivery routes.

The reality check

Qubits are fragile and error-prone, so much of the current work is about error correction. Quantum computers will not replace your laptop. They will be specialist tools used alongside normal computers.
